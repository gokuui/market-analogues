Case-Based Market Memory

For a stock chart known at time T, retrieve genuinely similar episodes that ended before T, reveal what happened after each one, and summarize the historical evidence with uncertainty and explicit reasons to abstain.

Product question: “What has the market previously done from states like this—and how consistent, independent, and relevant is that history?”

The intended learning loop

Experienced traders build memory by studying thousands of charts, comparing present development with prior development, observing successes and failures, and learning when context changes the outcome. This system makes that case-based process reproducible; it does not need to call a setup VCP, cup, flag, or anything else.

1. Observe today
Only information available at the chosen decision timestamp.
2. Recall history
Retrieve diverse, causally earlier chart states.
3. Explain similarity
Price, structure, volume, relative strength and regime.
4. Replay futures
Normalized paths, returns, MFE, MAE and failure routes.
5. Calibrate evidence
Sample independence, uncertainty, novelty and abstention.

What “perfect” must mean here

No historical system can promise that a chart “will grow.” Markets change, analogues are dependent, and rare events remain possible. A trustworthy system is instead causal, exhaustive within its declared universe, falsifiable, calibrated, explainable, and willing to say “insufficient evidence.”

Allowed conclusion“Among 34 independent close analogues, 21 reached +2 ATR before −1 ATR over 20 sessions; the interval is wide and evidence weakens outside risk-on regimes.”
Forbidden conclusion“This pattern has 62% confidence and will rise,” when the matches are overlapping, future-contaminated, regime-shifted, or selected after trying many definitions.

Outcome replay—not one arbitrary “win” label

Each historical analogue produces a future path normalized to the query’s scale. The system stores observations, not a conclusion:

  • Forward close return and benchmark-relative return at 5, 10, 20, 40, 60 and 126 sessions.
  • Maximum favorable and adverse excursion in return, ATR and structural-risk units.
  • Time to +1/+2/+3 ATR and −1/−2 ATR; which barrier was reached first.
  • Maximum drawdown before the best advance, gap failure, failed follow-through and recovery time.
  • Complete normalized future trajectory so users can see paths that share an endpoint but carry very different risk.
  • Censoring flags whenever a horizon is incomplete. A censored episode never enters that horizon’s denominator.

Critical causality rule: for a historical query at time T and outcome horizon H, an analogue is outcome-eligible only if its entire H-session future was already observable before T. Retrieval may use a newer analogue, but that analogue cannot contribute to a falsely historical outcome estimate.

The evidence card presented to a user

  1. Query snapshot: symbol, cutoff, resolution, market/sector state and data quality.
  2. Closest historical cases: aligned charts, distance, component explanation, date/symbol diversity and novelty.
  3. What happened next: overlaid normalized future paths plus a sortable table of every analogue.
  4. Outcome distribution: median, quartiles, tails, MFE/MAE, barrier order and censored counts.
  5. Counterexamples: the closest failures must be as visible as successes.
  6. Context sensitivity: same-regime, different-regime, same-sector and cross-sector panels.
  7. Evidence grade: based on closeness, effective sample size, stability, data quality and validated calibration—not on whether the result is bullish.
  8. Boundary: “historical descriptive evidence, not a recommendation,” with reproducible source/config hashes.

M07 — Walk-forward calibration and falsification

Protocol: expanding-window folds. At each query date, use only prior representations, prior completed outcomes and the then-valid universe. Lock development, validation and final test periods plus a trial ledger.

Baselines: unconditional market/sector frequency, regime-only frequency, return-matched random neighbors, price-only similarity and the current composite.

Metrics: Brier/log loss for barrier events; reliability diagrams and expected calibration error; interval coverage/width and pinball loss; median/path error; abstention coverage; regime and calendar stability.

Pass: no promotion from hit rate alone. The analogue system must improve locked proper-scoring metrics over simple baselines, maintain coverage on the final untouched period, and fail visibly if calibration breaks under shift.

M08 — Evidence report and API

Build: self-contained HTML/Excel/API evidence card with raw analogues, aligned charts, future paths, counterexamples, uncertainty, provenance and plain-language limitations.

Tests: every displayed number traces to stored rows; links and exports agree; absent data cannot render as zero; deterministic snapshot tests.

Pass: a user can reproduce the query and audit every contributing case without reading source code.

M09 — Shadow operation before decision use

Run: timestamp and freeze daily reports before future bars arrive. Never revise a report after its outcome. Monitor drift, novelty, calibration, latency and data failures.

Pass: sustained out-of-sample calibration and operational reliability across multiple regimes. Trading costs and explicit decision rules are a later, separately preregistered evaluation.

Research translated into design decisions

Primary evidenceDesign consequence
Kang et al., Déjà vu: forecasting through cross-similaritySupports retrieving cross-series analogues and aggregating their future paths, while evaluating both point behavior and interval uncertainty.
Zhao & Itti, shapeDTWUse local shape context and constrained alignment; raw pointwise DTW is not a complete chart-state measure.
UCR archive and STUMPYKeep independent generic baselines and exact motif/search implementations; a finance-specific composite is not presumed universally superior.
Murphy et al., metamorphic testingUse transformations and known relations when no human label or perfect real-data oracle exists.
Shumway, delisting biasOutcome claims require explicit delisting and survivorship accounting; free current-ticker history is insufficient for a production claim.
Bailey et al., backtest overfittingFreeze folds, metrics and trial counts. Repeatedly changing similarity/outcome definitions against the same history invalidates performance evidence.
Zaffran et al., adaptive conformal prediction for time seriesPrediction intervals under dependence and shift need time-aware calibration; ordinary exchangeable conformal claims are not imported blindly.
Retzlaff et al., coverage tests for non-stationary seriesTest marginal and conditional interval coverage formally; nominal coverage alone is not proof of calibration.

Final success definition

The system succeeds when it can say, reproducibly:

“These are the closest causally valid, diverse historical chart states. Here is exactly why they resemble today. Here are all their subsequent paths, including failures. The effective evidence is N, the current state is/is not novel, these outcome intervals have passed/failed walk-forward calibration, and this is where the evidence stops.”

That is the computerized equivalent of disciplined chart study. It is useful even when the answer is neutral or uncertain.
