What prior research gives us—and what it does not

An auditable evidence map for building point-in-time retrieval of historical stock developments. Each useful result is tied to a proposed experiment, implementation choice, or safeguard.

Primary sourcesPositive + negative findings54 evidence entriesNo profitability assumption

The honest answer

The idea is grounded in established work, but no paper supplies the complete system we want. Prior research separately supports generic historical matching, price/volume comparison, elastic alignment, multiscale and event-based representations, motif discovery, and scalable nearest-neighbor search. The contribution here is to combine them causally and evaluate similarity itself before evaluating outcomes.

Research-backed starting point: preserve an immutable point-in-time episode; generate several complementary views; retrieve coarsely; re-rank with constrained, interpretable alignment; validate against blinded trader judgments; only then inspect post-cutoff outcomes.
Coverage statement: this is a structured, reproducible technical review—not a claim that every paper ever published has been found. “All papers under the sun” is not provable. Coverage becomes defensible through an explicit query ledger, backward/forward citation chasing, duplicate removal, extraction records, and a saturation rule. The registry remains open when new methods or contrary results appear.

How the literature search is made auditable

The review is organized by research question rather than by whichever method is fashionable. Publisher pages, proceedings, author/institution repositories, NBER, the New York Fed, and official preprints are used as primary sources. Search-engine snippets are discovery aids only.

RQ1 · Financial precedent

Has chart structure or generic historical matching conveyed information in equities, indices, or adjacent liquid markets?

RQ2 · Information preservation

Which representations preserve price path, candles, volume, timing, shocks, complexity, and multiple scales?

RQ3 · Similarity

Which distances tolerate harmless phase changes without erasing economically important amplitude, duration, or ordering?

RQ4 · Multivariate context

How should stock, volume, relative strength, market, sector, and quality channels align?

RQ5 · Search at scale

How can millions of episodes be searched while measuring the candidate index's recall against exact search?

RQ6 · Scientific validity

How do survivorship, delisting, future adjustments, overlapping windows, repeated trials, and costs distort results?

Search families recorded

Inclusion and extraction rules

  1. Include a primary paper when it defines a reusable method, evaluates financial chart matching, supplies an important negative finding, or controls a material validity/scaling risk.
  2. Extract: data/domain, cutoff semantics when stated, representation, comparator, principal result, limitation, and the exact build/test implication.
  3. Do not transfer a benchmark win from speech, medicine, or UCR classification directly into finance. Such a method earns only a challenger experiment.
  4. Do not equate conditional-return differences, prediction accuracy, and realizable profit. They are separate evidence levels.
  5. Stop a search family only after two consecutive rounds of backward/forward citation chasing add no new method family or materially contrary result. Re-open on each major design change.

What the evidence already decides

Generic analogues are legitimate

Tsinaslanidis (2018, 2021) directly studies description-free historical price/volume matching. This is much closer to our question than named-pattern classification.

They are not automatically profitable

PIP+DTW on 18 equity indices supported weak-form efficiency; equity head-and-shoulders evidence was unprofitable; other studies find only bearish or conditional effects.

No universal best representation exists

Comparative time-series studies warn that proposed wins can be narrow. Required invariances depend on the domain, so we benchmark a portfolio of views.

Constrained warping, not free warping

DTW is useful for unequal development speed, but unconstrained paths can make nonsense alignments. Local-shape descriptors, stiffness, and warp diagnostics are mandatory.

Price alone is insufficient

Direct financial studies use price and volume; trader interpretation also depends on prior trend, volatility contraction, liquidity, relative strength, and market regime.

Similarity must be tested directly

Return labels cannot define the representation without turning retrieval into disguised prediction. Blinded human relevance and synthetic invariance tests come first.

What the literature does not settle

No reviewed paper proves that a single representation jointly captures daily OHLC candle anatomy, volume dry-ups/expansions, shocks, multi-horizon stock structure, relative strength, sector path, broad-market regime, and point-in-time cross-sectional ranks for historical-equity analogue retrieval.
  • The target notion of “looks similar to a professional trader” has no standard benchmark. We must create a blinded relevance set and publish its agreement/ambiguity.
  • Corporate-action point-in-time chart appearance is under-specified. We must prove invariance to later adjustments or reconstruct cutoff-known adjustments.
  • Most generic representation papers optimize classification, not retrieval relevance. Their headline accuracy cannot choose our method.
  • Financial pattern studies use different universes, eras, exits, and costs. Their economic results are not directly combinable.
  • Market context may be part of similarity or a filter. Both must be evaluated; one global score should not be assumed.
  • Overlapping episodes create pseudo-replication. Uncertainty must cluster by security and calendar block, and neighbor diversity must prevent one long run from flooding results.

Research completion and maintenance gate

A research family is ready for implementation only when its ledger has the queries, dates, databases, included/excluded primary sources with reasons, backward/forward citation pass, extracted result, limitation, and task/test link. The implementation can begin before the entire literature is frozen, but no method can be promoted without its evidence record.

  1. Initial saturation: two citation-chasing rounds add no new method family or contrary conclusion.
  2. Reproduction: implement the strongest transparent baseline from the paper, not only our variant.
  3. Transfer test: compare on the same causal stock benchmark and same candidate pool.
  4. Decision record: adopt, retain as challenger, defer, or reject—with measured reason.
  5. Quarterly refresh: rerun saved queries and review new citing work; immediate refresh when a design assumption changes.
Definition of research “done” for version one: all core rows have primary-source verification and task/test traceability; every selected method has a faithful baseline; every rejected core alternative has a recorded benchmark reason; open gaps are explicit. This is defensible completeness, not an impossible claim of universal completeness.
