The decision

We should not choose one magical representation such as a wave, a string, or DTW. Each solves a different part of the problem.

Keep the exact historical episode intact, then create several complementary views of it: the raw path, the swing structure, the behavior at different time scales, and the market context.

This gives us two protections:

A

We do not lose the original chart

Every match can be traced back to the exact OHLCV, market, sector, and data-quality observations available at the cutoff.

B

We do not trust one mathematical opinion

Dense paths, swing geometry, elastic timing, and market context can agree or disagree—and the interface shows those scores separately.

What “the complete stock picture” means

The representation must preserve what the trader could have known at that moment, across several chart zoom levels.

1

Price behavior

Open, high, low, close, overnight gaps, candle bodies and wicks, closes near highs/lows, moving-average structure, distance from prior highs, and the prior advance.

2

Shocks and participation

Unusual returns, gaps, true-range expansion, relative volume, up-volume versus down-volume, dry-ups, liquidity, and whether a shock held or was rejected.

3

Structure over time

Advances, pullbacks, recoveries, tightening, shakeouts, contraction ratios, duration, volatility compression, expansion, and coiling near a boundary.

4

Relative strength

Stock versus NASDAQ, broad market, and sector; leadership percentile; whether relative strength improves before price; and whether the sector itself is strengthening.

5

Market environment

QQQ/IXIC, SPY, IWM, sector paths, volatility, drawdown, breadth, new highs/lows, distribution, and cross-sectional participation—not only one “bull/bear” flag.

6

Several zoom levels

21, 63, 126, and 252 daily sessions plus two years of weekly structure, all ending at the same information cutoff.

Important boundary: earnings and news catalysts are not visible in OHLCV alone. They can be added later as clearly marked optional context, especially for EP searches.

How the system works

The process is deliberately split so representation, similarity, and outcomes cannot leak into each other.

1. Exact episodePoint-in-time stock, market, sector, volume, metadata, and quality.
2. Multiple viewsDense paths, swing skeleton, multiscale behavior, and context.
3. Fast searchFind a few hundred plausible candidates across history.
4. Careful re-rankCompare structure and allow limited differences in development speed.
5. Show outcomesOverlay the matches and reveal only futures already knowable at the query date.

What the user sees

  • The current chart and 10–20 historical analogues ending at clearly marked cutoffs.
  • Separate scores for price shape, volume, swing structure, relative strength, market, sector, and timing warp.
  • Shape-only results and market-context-matched results as separate views.
  • Subsequent paths, MFE, MAE, target-before-stop, and failed analogues—not only winners.
  • A plain explanation: “smaller contractions, drying volume, improving relative strength, but a weaker market than today.”

Roadmap with proof gates

Each phase must prove something before more sophistication is added.

0

Make the historical record trustworthy

Stable security IDs, ticker changes, active and delisted universe, corporate actions, volume adjustment, bar-quality flags, indices, sectors, and breadth.

Gate: randomly selected historical charts reproduce exactly with no future inputs.
1

Build the canonical episode

Create causal stock, volume, relative-strength, market, sector, quality, and multiscale views. Render the same episode for human review.

Gate: split, gap, missing-bar and rolling-window tests pass.
2

Establish the simplest retrieval baseline

Compare close-only, full OHLC, OHLC+volume, OHLC+volume+RS, and complete context-aware paths.

Gate: added information improves blind chart-similarity judgments.
3

Add swing structure and limited time flexibility

Test constrained DTW, derivative/shape-aware DTW, multivariate alignment, and multithreshold swing skeletons.

Gate: better held-out human ranking justifies the latency and complexity.
4

Challenge the baseline

Test wavelets, symbolic strings, Matrix Profile and change-point sequences. Reject anything that does not add relevance, stability, or speed.

Gate: freeze representation version 1 before examining outcome advantage.
5

Build the analogue explorer

Scale the historical index; show synchronized overlays, component scores, alignments, context tabs and future-path distributions.

Gate: usable query speed and repeatable, explainable neighbors.
6

Study outcomes and discover families

Compare mature neighbor outcomes with matched controls. Only then cluster unnamed setup families or test learned similarity.

Gate: walk-forward evidence survives regimes, costs and research-selection controls.

Non-negotiable guardrails

These matter more than choosing a clever distance formula.

No future barsEvery episode ends exactly at its declared decision time.
Mature outcomes onlyAt a historical query date, a 60-day outcome is usable only if those 60 sessions had already elapsed.
No survivorship biasFailed, acquired and delisted securities remain in the search universe.
No winner-built similarityTrader similarity—not subsequent return—chooses the representation and weights.
No silent bad dataBar-level quality, missingness and corrections remain visible to the matcher and user.
No duplicate dominationOverlapping windows and repeated episodes from one stock are collapsed or capped.
No fake US-wide claimNASDAQ is only one exchange. Add NYSE and NYSE American before calling the universe all US stocks.
No hidden similarity scoreKeep shape, volume, structure, RS, market, sector and time-warp contributions inspectable.

Recommended choices for approval

These defaults allow implementation to begin without closing off later research.

  1. Decision time: begin with after-close daily episodes. Treat gap-open/EP searches as a distinct later episode type.
  2. History: search 126- and 252-session developments, emphasize the recent 21/63 days, and retain two years of weekly context.
  3. Universe: a NASDAQ-only proof of concept is acceptable; production should cover major US exchanges, including delisted stocks.
  4. Context: retrieve stock-shape candidates first, then show context-matched results separately instead of hiding market context inside one score.
  5. First matcher: fast fixed-vector search followed by swing-structure and constrained-DTW re-ranking.
  6. Ground truth: use blind trader pair judgments to decide what “similar” means. Hide outcomes during that review.
  7. Discovery: do not name or cluster setup families until representation version 1 is frozen.

Expandable technical appendix

Exact representation layers
  1. Lossless source: raw/adjusted OHLCV, adjustment factors, session status, security metadata, indices, sector, breadth and quality provenance.
  2. Dense trajectories: candle mechanics, gaps, return/range shocks, volume, compression, moving-average relationships, relative paths and ranks.
  3. Structural skeleton: pivots at several prominence levels, swing amplitude/duration, retracements, contraction ratios and price-volume behavior.
  4. Time-scale descriptor: controlled wavelet/scattering summaries for returns, range, relative volume, RS and benchmark paths.
  5. Context trajectory: broad market, NASDAQ, small-cap, sector, breadth, volatility and participation as separate inspectable channels.
How “best representation” will be tested
  • Build 300–500 query charts: known examples ending before success, random dates, failures, different eras, regimes and liquidity groups.
  • Ask blind 0–4 judgments for overall development, swings, endpoint, shock, volume, RS and market-context similarity.
  • Use pairwise questions—“which candidate is closer?”—because they are easier to answer consistently.
  • Test harmless transformations: nominal price/volume scaling, legitimate splits, tiny timing shifts and small time stretches.
  • Test meaningful changes: materially deeper pullback, reversed RS, reordered shocks and different market behavior.
  • Measure human ranking agreement, precision at K, stability, sensitivity, diversity, latency, index size and explanation quality.
  • Reject sophistication that does not improve held-out human relevance or speed.

Repository-specific data work required
  • Introduce stable security identity across ticker changes and ticker reuse.
  • Audit active/delisted membership and security type at each date.
  • Audit volume adjustment against adjusted OHLC and preserve raw adjustment factors.
  • Add QQQ, SPY, IWM, sector/industry proxies and point-in-time breadth; IXIC alone is insufficient.
  • Keep bar-level quality flags. Previous NASDAQ experiments documented bad-symbol and delisting artifacts that materially changed results.
  • Separate the representation store from the outcome store so future information is unavailable by construction.
