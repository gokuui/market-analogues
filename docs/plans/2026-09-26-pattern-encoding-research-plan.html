Pattern encoding research plan

market-analogues · 2026-09-26 · Follows the Step 1–3 decision run (experiments/decision/LOCAL_RUN_RESULTS.md). Based on four parallel literature reviews using Paper Lantern, Exa and MarketMaker.

Question. The owner believes chart patterns carry predictive information and that the analogue engine failed because of how it encodes charts, not because patterns are meaningless. This plan checks that belief against published research. It proposes better encodings and a fast, honest leaderboard for ranking them.

1. What the research says

The belief is partly right

Chart shape does carry information beyond standard features. The strongest evidence is Jiang, Kelly & Xiu, (Re-)Imag(in)ing Price Trends (J. Finance 2023):

  • Setup: a CNN trained on 5/20/60-day OHLC + volume + moving-average images of US stocks.
  • Result: it beat momentum and reversal. Known signals explain only about 10% of its forecasts.
  • Source of the gain: the authors' ablation shows it comes from the representation. The window is rescaled to its own high–low range, and the model is trained on the forward outcome. A 1D CNN on the same scaled series matches the 2D image model.

Why the current engine is the wrong encoding

Current engineWhat the evidence favours
Whole 252-session windowLocal windows of 5–60 bars, aligned on the event (breakout or gap day)
Hand-designed "looks similar" distance (unsupervised)Similarity learned from forward outcomes (JKX; FASCL 2026; Choi & Cho 2026)
Average of 20 neighbours, no shrinkageSupervised prediction head. Retrieval kept for explanation, with hub control and shrinkage
Scores probability (3-class Brier) on 1,700 queriesCross-sectional ranking on millions of stock-days, then payoff (R-multiples) with stops

The closest published version of the current system is Tsinaslanidis & Kugiumtzis (2014), which combined perceptually important points with DTW analogue matching. It did not beat the efficient-market baseline on any of 18 equity indices, the same outcome found here. Every positive result in this area is supervised, short-horizon (≤20 days), in-sample from the 1990s, or outside finance.

The "chart as a string" idea

  • No evidence Generic symbolic strings (SAX, iSAX, ABBA, candlestick symbols, matrix-profile motifs): no predictive evidence for stock returns. They are compression and indexing tools.
  • Untested A string of swings: the base as a sequence of legs, each with depth in ATR units, duration, volume ratio and slope. This is the practitioner's VCP grammar, and nobody has tested it rigorously. It is cheap to test.

Payoff, not accuracy. Pattern edges show up in the size of wins relative to losses under a stop:
  • O'Neil breakouts have a flat 57% hit rate, but filtering changes average gain versus loss.
  • Han, Zhou & Zhu: a 10% stop raised momentum's return from 0.99% to 1.69% a month.
An evaluation that scores only direction or probability will under-rate pattern information. The harness below therefore ends with a payoff test.

Also run first: a zero-cost diagnostic on the existing engine (D0 in phase P0 below).

  • If ranking skill is above chance, the negative Brier came from poor calibration, not from zero information.

3. Evaluation harness: rank many encodings quickly and honestly

Why the old test was too weak

The old test scored 1,700 hand-selected queries, and each query outcome carries full stock-level noise. It would need 99 months of live data to confirm a 1.9-point effect. The new harness scores every stock on every sampled date, then compares models date by date, so shared market noise cancels out. The agent's illustrative arithmetic: with σΔIC ≈ 0.03 over about 1,000 dates, it can detect a gain in rank IC of about 0.003 using historical data alone.

Specification

  • Label store (frozen, hashed):
    • Entry at the next open.
    • Forward open-to-open return over 5, 10 and 20 days, as excess over the market, ranked within each date.
    • Binary "top quintile" label.
    • The old barrier label, kept for continuity.
  • Universe: point-in-time price and liquidity floors, at least 250 days of history, and delisted stocks included.
  • Missing delisting returns: substitute −30% (Shumway) or −55% for NASDAQ (Shumway & Warther); −50% for NSE.
  • NSE circuit-locked bars: not fillable, so excluded as entries.
  • Primary score: ΔICt = RankIC(GBM + X) − RankIC(GBM) per date, with a Newey–West t-statistic on the mean. This is the Diebold–Mariano-style test from Gu, Kelly & Xiu.
  • One common evaluator: every encoding becomes a per-(stock, date) vector. It is scored by Probe A (PCA-32 + ridge) and Probe B (a fixed-hyperparameter LightGBM on the 22 features + PCA-16 of X). Probe B's ΔIC is the leaderboard score. Baseline predictions are cached, so each candidate costs one embedding pass and two fits per fold.
  • Protocol:
    • Expanding walk-forward with yearly refits, test years 2006–2023.
    • Purge and embargo (h + 5 days).
    • Every learned encoder is trained only on data from before each fold's cutoff.
  • Vault: 2024-01 onward (NASDAQ to 2026-03, NSE to 2026-02). Opened once, only for the 3–5 finalists, after the leaderboard is frozen.
  • Secondary metrics: residual IC (a spanning test), a Fama–MacBeth coefficient, top-decile net excess return (costs from Corwin–Schultz / Abdi–Ranaldo spreads, 15 bp per side minimum for NSE), percentage of positive years, US-versus-India sign agreement, and kNN neighbour consistency and calibration.
  • Multiple testing:
    • Every trial is logged: distance, window and seed each count.
    • Holm correction across the family, plus Hansen's SPA test and the Model Confidence Set, both from arch.bootstrap.
    • t ≥ 3 hurdle (Harvey–Liu–Zhu).
    • Deflated Sharpe ratio and PBO/CSCV for finalist portfolios.

Stage 2 for finalists: payoff test

  • Use each finalist as a filter on the entries of existing strategies (loser gen498, gen182/191, VCP+RS, 2015–2026).
  • Also run an event study with a fixed exit policy: structural stop at ≤ 1–1.5×ADR, partial profit at 2R, trailing exit on the 10/20-day MA.
  • Report the R-multiple distribution, expectancy and profit factor, against a null of matched random entries.
  • Pass: Calmar (return ÷ maximum drawdown) improves after costs in at least 4 of 5 eras.

4. Execution order

  1. P0 — Harness and baseline (1–2 days). Build the label store, universe masks and cost model. Cache walk-forward baseline predictions (E0). Build the leaderboard writer (schema below). Run the D0 diagnostic on the existing engine. Gate: synthetic-leak tests pass (a future-peeking feature must be caught), and baseline ΔIC against itself is 0.
  2. P1 — Cheap encodings E1 and E2 (1–2 days). This answers directly whether the specific pattern properties add anything beyond the 22 features.
  3. P2 — Supervised chart model E3/E4 (2–4 days). Pilot on 20% of stocks first; promote to a full run only if the pilot t > 1.5.
  4. P3 — Retrieval rescue E5 (2 days). Keeps the "what happened to similar charts" view if it is calibrated.
  5. P4 — E6, only if budget allows.
  6. P5 — Freeze the leaderboard, open the vault, then run the payoff test on finalists.

Stop rule for the whole program: if nothing reaches "Promising" by P3, chart shape adds no usable information beyond simple features on daily bars in this data. Record that and archive the project.

6. Honest expectations

  • The published signal is largest at 1–5 days, in small and illiquid stocks, and in equal-weighted portfolios. It shrinks at 20 days and after costs.
  • Transfer across markets is mixed: a US-trained model gave about zero in one China replication. Train and test on NSE separately.
  • India ranked among the weakest of 31 markets in one pattern study (Lee et al. 2019).
  • Realistic hope: a small gain over the GBM that is real and consistent, plus better entries through payoff filtering. A breakthrough is unlikely.
