T14-11 — Verified Stockbee 25% / 4% controlled study

Result: Early 4% moves are substantially more common in stocks undergoing a 25% advance than in same-date controls matched on prior return, volatility, price and liquidity. The result survives calendar-month block inference and Holm correction, but the literal exact-start majority claim does not.

What was tested

The locked NASDAQ archive contributed 37,358,636 eligible symbol/horizon starts. Consecutive 25%-winner windows were consolidated into 528,114 event runs. Broad and investable analyses together produced 710,373 event/population rows and 3,551,826 deterministic control links.

All 32 primary effects are positive and Holm-significant at adjusted p=0.0032. Ten also clear the stricter frozen claim gate, which additionally requires winner prevalence of at least 50%, risk ratio above one, at least 90% matching coverage and agreement across broad/investable directions.

What this supports

  • For one-month 25% advances, a +4% close day appears during the first five sessions in a clear majority of both broad and investable events.
  • Bullish range expansion narrowly exceeds 50% for those one-month events.
  • The association remains large after causal same-date matching.

What it does not support

  • Only 13–26% of events contain the studied exposure on the exact first day; therefore a majority do not literally begin that way.
  • The investable 63-session winner prevalence is below 50%, despite being materially higher than controls.
  • This is not yet a buy signal. The 4% day is part of the same window used to label the 25% advance, so the retrospective association is partly mechanical.

Next experiment: predictive inversion

Freeze the close of the first qualifying 4% day as the signal timestamp. Starting with the next session, measure 5/20/60-session absolute and benchmark-relative returns, favourable/adverse excursions and barriers. Match against same-date stocks using only information known at the signal, including NASDAQ market regime. Only a verified post-signal advantage should be incorporated into analogue retrieval.
